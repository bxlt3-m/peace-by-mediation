Ten years of Nobel prizes, and the Algerian mediator who deserves one

A decade of Nobel Peace Prizes, the science honoured beside them, and an argument for Lakhdar Brahimi, the Algerian diplomat who helped broker the Taif Agreement and chaired the Bonn conference.

01 · Peace prizes

Ten Peace Prizes, and what they reward

Only two of the ten, Colombia in 2016 and Ethiopia in 2019, rewarded a negotiated end to a specific conflict. The rest went to people who document, testify and campaign: on nuclear weapons, press freedom, hunger, sexual violence and democracy.2

02 · Science

Four science prizes from the last two years

Each entry names the laureates and says what they actually showed.2

Chemistry 2025

Metal-organic frameworks

Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson and Omar Yaghi built crystalline materials full of tiny pores. The pores can trap gases, pull water out of desert air and filter pollutants.

Physics 2025

Quantum tunnelling in an electrical circuit

John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis showed that a circuit large enough to hold in your hand can follow quantum rules, tunnelling through a barrier and absorbing energy in fixed steps. Superconducting quantum computers build on that result.

Medicine 2025

Peripheral immune tolerance

Mary Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell and Shimon Sakaguchi identified how regulatory T cells keep the immune system from attacking the body's own tissue. The work feeds research on autoimmune disease, cancer treatment and organ transplants.

Chemistry 2024

Designing and predicting proteins

David Baker used computation to design entirely new proteins. Demis Hassabis and John Jumper built AlphaFold, which predicts a protein's three-dimensional structure from its amino acid sequence.

03 · The Algerian case

Lakhdar Brahimi and the case for a Peace Prize

Brahimi was born in 1934 in El Azizia, near Tablat, Algeria. In 1956, two years into the war of independence, he left his studies in Paris to join the liberation struggle. He was 22.3 His last major mission ended in 2014, when he resigned as the joint UN and Arab League envoy for Syria.

The Nobel Foundation keeps nominations secret for 50 years, so nobody outside the committee can say who has or has not been nominated (Nobel Foundation). What follows is an argument for why he deserves the prize, not a claim that he is a candidate.

1

He has produced signed agreements

As the Arab League's envoy on Lebanon he worked with the committee of Saudi Arabia, Morocco and Algeria that led to the Taif Agreement, signed on 22 October 1989. It ended fifteen years of civil war.12

He was the UN's representative in South Africa before the first post-apartheid elections in 1994, and he chaired the Bonn conference in November and December 2001. The agreement signed there on 5 December set up an interim authority under Hamid Karzai.15

2

He changed how the UN plans peace operations

Kofi Annan convened his panel on 7 March 2000, and it reported on 17 August. The Brahimi Report asked for more robust mandates, a stronger planning and support capacity at headquarters, and deployment within 30 days for traditional missions and 90 days for complex ones. Annan called its recommendations essential if the UN was to be credible as a force for peace.9,10

The Security Council acted on several points in Resolution 1327 later that year. A 2003 Stimson Center review found most recommendations had been put into practice, with operational ones moving faster than those on doctrine and strategy.

3

He has negotiated as a rebel, a minister and an envoy

He represented the FLN in Southeast Asia from Jakarta between 1956 and 1961. He later served Algeria as ambassador and as foreign minister from 1991 to 1993, and the Arab League as under-secretary-general.3

His UN postings include Haiti, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, South Africa, Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria.4

4

Peace institutions have recognised the work

He received the Dag Hammarskjöld Medal of Honor in 2004, the Hesse Peace Prize in 2006 and the Wateler Peace Prize in 2014.3 Harvard's Program on Negotiation gave him its Great Negotiator Award in 2002 for the Afghanistan talks.17

He was a founding member of The Elders in 2007 and became an Elder Emeritus in 2021.3

The other side

What counts against him

A fair case has to include the places where the record is weak.

Syria, 2012 to 2014

He resigned after nearly two years without a settlement.18 The Geneva II talks he chaired ended in February 2014 with no breakthrough, and Ban Ki-moon called his departure a tragedy for the Syrian people and a failure for the UN.19

Afghanistan, 2001

The Bonn conference left the Taliban out. Brahimi told Radio Free Europe they would not have attended and the Americans would not have allowed it, though he also pointed to shortcomings in the agreement.

Lebanon, 1989

Taif ended the fighting but left the sectarian system largely in place. The analyst Bahout, quoted in The National, said it did little more than readjust the old order, and the Lebanese statesman Raymond Edde warned at the time that it was fatally flawed.

Algeria, 1991 to 1993

Brahimi was foreign minister when the army cancelled elections that an Islamic party was poised to win. A biographical profile on Salaam.co.uk says his justification of that move has remained a stain on his career. He left Algeria two years later and joined the UN.

04 · Assessment

Where the case stands

The committee has honoured mediators and peacekeeping before: Ralph Bunche in 1950, the United Nations together with Kofi Annan in 2001, and Martti Ahtisaari in 2008.2 Brahimi's work on UN peace operations connects directly to the second of those. Against him, recent prizes lean toward advocates and institutions rather than negotiators, and Abiy's 2019 prize was followed by war in Tigray from November 2020.

His record is also mixed in the places that matter most to the argument. Syria failed, and both Bonn and Taif have been criticised for what they left unresolved. The honest version of the case is narrower than a list of agreements. It rests on the craft of mediation and on the Brahimi Report, not on any single settlement.

Nobody can confirm whether the committee has ever considered him. Nomination records for a given year open only after 50 years.1
